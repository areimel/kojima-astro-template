A synthetic notice reports reduced handling capacity at the fictional Port Meridian. A separate supplier update mentions a delayed dispatch. These records are an exercise in corporate decision support, not a report of a real disruption.

## Begin with the decision

The sample team needs to decide whether to review an alternate route for a time-sensitive component. That question gives the review a boundary: which shipments use the corridor, when they are due, and whether an alternative is feasible.

A broad regional headline alone cannot answer it. The first task is to connect the notice to a specific business dependency.

## Separate the records

| Record                    | What it says                               | What remains unknown                      |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Port notice / SYN-001     | Capacity is reduced during a stated window | Whether the relevant terminal is affected |
| Supplier update / SYN-002 | Dispatch is later than planned             | Whether the cause is related to the port  |
| Route plan / SYN-003      | The sample shipment uses this corridor     | Whether the route plan is still current   |

Each record needs an observation time, a reporting time, and a source reference. Two reports repeating the same notice should not count as independent confirmation.

## Write a bounded assessment

A useful sample assessment reads: “The shipment may be exposed to a delay if its terminal and handling window overlap the notice. The supplier update does not yet establish a shared cause.”

This phrasing keeps the possible business impact visible without turning a correlation into a confirmed incident.

## Close with a next step

The brief asks a logistics reviewer to confirm the terminal, obtain the current dispatch estimate, and compare the alternative route against the delivery requirement. It also states when to revisit the assessment.

Kojima demonstrates how that reasoning could be presented. This template does not ingest port notices, track shipments, or execute routing decisions.
