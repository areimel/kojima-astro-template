This synthetic exercise follows a fictional component supplier, Aster Works, and a fictional logistics corridor, North Passage. The purpose is to explore how a corporate risk team might turn a regional signal into a focused exposure review.

## A location match is a starting point

A supplier address inside an affected region does not prove that a shipment, facility, or production line is disrupted. The entity might have several sites. A shipment might use a different route.

The sample review records the exact dependency behind each connection:

- Which supplier site makes the component?
- Which route carries the relevant shipment?
- Which internal operation depends on that delivery?
- When was each relationship last checked?

## Keep assumptions attached to the map

Suppose a sample relationship says Aster Works ships through North Passage. Label the source and date of that relationship. If it comes from an old route plan, mark it as requiring confirmation.

A diagram is useful when a reviewer can understand why an edge exists. An unexplained line can make a weak assumption look authoritative.

## Distinguish exposure from impact

In this exercise, exposure means a dependency could intersect with the event. Impact requires additional evidence about what has actually changed.

The review might classify the route as “potentially exposed” while leaving the delivery impact “unconfirmed.” Those two states should remain visible together.

## Escalate with a specific request

The sample handoff asks the logistics owner to validate the current route and the supplier owner to confirm dispatch timing. It includes the affected dependency and the unanswered question, rather than a general request to investigate the region.

These are proposed review practices for the Kojima demo. No real supplier records, live transport data, or automated escalation service are included.
